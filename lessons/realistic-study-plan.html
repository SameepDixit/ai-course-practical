No. 6.04Track 6 · Teachers and students

Make a study plan you can actually follow

Lesson 4 of 55 min read1 task · 3 questions

Tell the AI your days, your hours and your weak subject. Get a plan with room to breathe.

Done

AThe idea

Many study plans fail in the first week. They pack too much into each day and leave no time for when things go wrong. A realistic plan starts from how many days you have and how many hours you can really study each day, after school and tuition.

Give the AI your subjects and tell it which one is weak. Ask for more time on that subject. Ask for revision days at the end, and one buffer day. A buffer day is an empty day to catch up if you fall sick or a day goes badly.

Ask for the plan as a simple day-by-day list. Copy it into Google Sheets or your notebook. Tick each day when you finish it. If you miss a day, use the buffer day. Do not give up on the whole plan.

BYour task

Plan 14 days of study for four subjects

Change the days, hours and subjects to yours. Paste the prompt.

Your slip: copy-paste prompt 
Make a study plan for my school exams.

- Days left: 14. The exam starts on day 15.
- I can study 3 hours a day, in two sessions of 1.5 hours.
- Subjects: Maths, Science, Social Science, English.
- Maths is my weakest subject. English is my strongest.
- Days 11 and 12 are revision days.
- Day 13 is a buffer day with nothing planned.
- Day 14 is light review only.

Format: a day-by-day list with the two subjects for each day. Then a count of sessions per subject. Keep it under 170 words.
