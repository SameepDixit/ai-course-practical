No. 6.03Track 6 · Teachers and students

Understand a hard topic at three levels, then test yourself

Lesson 3 of 55 min read1 task · 3 questions

Start simple, go deeper, end exam-ready. Then let the AI quiz you.

Done

AThe idea

A textbook explains a topic once, at one level. If that level is too hard, you get stuck. AI can explain the same idea again and again, in different ways, without getting tired.

Ask for three levels. First, explain it as if you are 12 years old. Then explain it like a Class 10 student. Last, give the exam-ready version: the definition and key points you would write in an answer. Each level builds on the one before.

Then ask the AI to quiz you, one question at a time, and to wait for your answer. Reading feels like learning. Answering questions is what shows you what you really know.

Always compare the exam-ready version with your textbook and your teacher's notes. Your school's way of writing the answer comes first.

BYour task

Learn Newton's third law of motion and answer 3 questions

Change the topic to one you find hard. Paste the prompt, then type your answer to each question.

Your slip: copy-paste prompt 
Explain Newton's third law of motion at three levels:
1. Like I am 12 years old (2-3 lines, one everyday example)
2. Like I am a Class 10 student (4-5 lines)
3. Exam-ready (the law in one line, plus 3 key points and 2 examples)

Use simple English.

Then quiz me with 3 questions, from easy to hard. Ask one question at a time. Wait for my answer. Tell me if I am right, and explain briefly if I am wrong. Then ask the next question.

Into the press

What you should get

A real example of a good result. Your wording will differ a little. The shape and the facts should match.

Printed resultExample

1. Like you are 12
When you push a wall, the wall pushes you back just as hard. That is why your hands feel the push.

2. Like a Class 10 student
Forces always come in pairs. When object A pushes object B, B pushes A back. The two forces are equal in size and opposite in direction. They act on two different objects, so they do not cancel each other.

3. Exam-ready
Law: To every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction.
Key points:
- Action and reaction are equal in size.
- They act in opposite directions.
- They act on different bodies, so they do not cancel.
Examples: A rocket pushes gases down, and the gases push the rocket up. A gun moves back (recoils) when it fires a bullet.

Quiz, question 1 of 3:
You jump off a small boat onto the shore. Which way does the boat move?
Type your answer.

Tap the sheet to print it all at once.

Why it works: Three levels let you climb from an easy picture to the exam answer, and the one-at-a-time quiz makes you recall it instead of only reading it.
